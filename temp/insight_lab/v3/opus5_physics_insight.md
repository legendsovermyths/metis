# [opus5] Drunkards Solved With Resistors

## Insight
Whether a random walker on an infinite lattice eventually comes home is not a probability question at all — it's an electrical one. Replace every lattice edge with a 1-ohm resistor; the walk is recurrent exactly when the resistance from the origin out to infinity is infinite, because the escape probability is literally 1/(deg · R_eff). And resistance obeys a brutally simple monotonicity: soldering nodes together (shorting) can only lower it, snipping edges can only raise it. So Pólya's theorem becomes a back-of-envelope circuit exercise: short together all lattice points at the same distance in the 2D grid and the series sum of ~1/n resistances diverges, so the 2D drunk always gets home; delete edges from the 3D grid until only a tree of disjoint escape paths remains and their parallel resistance still converges, so the 3D drunk is lost forever.

- **The turn:** A theorem about infinitely many random paths is decided by a crude bound on a resistor network — and 'shorting can only help, cutting can only hurt' is what makes the estimate free.
- **Domain:** probability / potential theory (random walks and electrical networks)
- **Taste match:** It's a self-contained clever trick of exactly the contest flavor — swap a hard object for an equivalent easy one — but the equivalence is a genuine theorem about the graph Laplacian, the same operator behind spanning-tree counts and harmonic functions he already meets in graph algorithms.
- **Level fit:** Assumes random walk recurrence, harmonic functions / discrete Laplacian, series-parallel resistance, and comfort with divergence of sum 1/n versus convergence of sum 1/n^2 — all within reach of Concrete Math + Erickson, nothing requiring measure theory.
- **Grounding:** Doyle & Snell, 'Random Walks and Electric Networks': voltages are harmonic and equal hitting probabilities; escape probability from a node v equals 1/(deg(v)·R_eff(v→∞)); Rayleigh's monotonicity law and the shorting/cutting method; this yields Pólya's 1921 theorem that simple random walk is recurrent on Z and Z^2, transient on Z^d for d≥3. Nash-Williams inequality is the cutting-law refinement.
- **Form hint:** teach — the payoff is the mechanism (the dictionary plus monotonicity), which needs explaining rather than guessing
