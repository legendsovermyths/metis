# [opus5] (puzzle) Equally Common, Unequally Awaited

## Insight
Flip a fair coin until you first see HH; then repeat until you first see HT. Expected flips: 6 for HH, but only 4 for HT — even though in n flips the expected *number* of HH occurrences and HT occurrences are identical, (n−1)/4 each, and successive occurrences of each are on average 4 flips apart. The extra 2 flips is the price of self-overlap: HH can reuse its own final H as the next occurrence's first H, so HH arrives in bursts (gaps of 1) separated by long droughts, while HT gaps are tame. Starting from scratch you land in a random gap, and random gaps are length-biased — you're more likely to fall inside a long one — so the wait exceeds the mean gap exactly for the clumpy pattern. (Check HT in your head: wait for the first H, 2 flips, then wait for the first T, 2 more, and no H is ever wasted.)

- **The turn:** Two patterns with identical frequency and identical average spacing have different expected waiting times — because one pattern overlaps itself and therefore clumps, and clumping inflates the gap you happen to be dropped into.
- **Domain:** discrete probability / pattern occurrence (renewal theory, Conway correlation)
- **Taste match:** It's a self-contained contest-flavoured trick (set up two equations, solve in your head) that turns out to rest on a real structural idea — a string's overlap with itself, the same autocorrelation that makes KMP failure functions work — which is exactly the algorithm-theory-under-the-recipe pleasure of Erickson and Concrete Mathematics.
- **Level fit:** The computation is a two-state first-passage recurrence he can do mentally; the *explanation* (length-biased sampling of inter-arrival gaps, self-overlap as autocorrelation) is graduate-flavoured but needs no machinery beyond what Concrete Mathematics already gives him.
- **Grounding:** Standard Markov first-passage computation: E[HH]=6, E[HT]=4. Conway's leading-number / correlation-polynomial formula: expected wait for pattern A over a fair binary alphabet equals the sum of 2^k over all k such that A's length-k prefix equals its length-k suffix (HH: 4+2=6; HT: 4+0=4). Expected occurrence count of any fixed 2-pattern in n flips is (n−1)/4, so the stationary mean inter-arrival gap is 4 for both (renewal-density / ergodic argument). The excess of first-passage time over mean gap is the inspection/length-bias effect, E[first wait] = E[G²]/(2E[G]) + ... in the discrete renewal setting; equivalently the gap distribution for HH has higher second moment due to overlap. See Guibas–Odlyzko, 'String overlaps, pattern matching, and nontransitive games' (1981).
- **Form hint:** puzzle — it poses as a clean 'which number is bigger, and by how much?' with objective answers 6 and 4, and the reveal is the mechanism behind the gap.
