# [gemini31] (story) Planck's Combinatorial Hack

## Insight
Max Planck didn't discover quantum mechanics by observing discrete particles; he stumbled into it as a desperate mathematical hack. To fix the infinite-energy bug in classical blackbody equations, he borrowed a trick from discrete math, dividing energy into countable chunks just so he could run combinatorics on them. His plan was to take the limit as the chunk size approached zero at the end of the proof, cleanly restoring continuous classical physics. But the math refused to resolve: the equation only matched reality if the discrete 'hack' remained permanent. Planck spent the next fifteen years trying to patch out his own discovery, horrified that his temporary bookkeeping trick had accidentally pixelated the universe.

- **The turn:** Quantum mechanics wasn't born from a profound physical realization, but as a temporary combinatorial trick that its own creator spent a decade trying to debug out of existence.
- **Domain:** History of Quantum Mechanics
- **Taste match:** As a competitive programmer and SWE, you know the feeling of using a discrete abstraction to approximate a system; this is the story of a discrete mathematical hack that turned out to be the true source code of reality.
- **Level fit:** It bridges your rigorous understanding of combinatorics from Concrete Mathematics with fundamental physics, showing how a purely discrete mathematical constraint forced a paradigm shift in physics.
- **Grounding:** Max Planck's 1900 derivation of the blackbody radiation law using discrete energy elements (E=hf) as a mathematical heuristic, and his prolonged resistance to Einstein's physical interpretation of the quantum.
- **Form hint:** story | A historical narrative where a discrete mathematical abstraction outsmarts its human creator.
