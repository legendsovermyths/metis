# [opus5] MATERIAL v2 — FFT Is Just CRT  ·  **puzzle**

_Why this form:_ The core of the insight can be posed as a concrete, objective computation — reduce a degree-<8 polynomial modulo x^4−1 and x^4+1 and read off the coefficients — whose answer *is* the butterfly, so the reader discovers the identification instead of being told it. There is no live disagreement here, only a hidden structural identity, so ARGUMENT would be artificial.

## Prerequisites (footing, defined in-document)
- **Polynomial ring R[x]** — The set of polynomials with coefficients in a ring R (for us R = ℂ, or any ring where 2 and n are invertible and a primitive nth root of unity exists), with the usual addition and multiplication. A polynomial of degree < n is just its coefficient vector (a_0, …, a_{n−1}).
- **Polynomial division with remainder / 'mod f(x)'** — For polynomials A and f with f monic, there are unique Q, R with A = Qf + R and deg R < deg f; R is written A mod f. Working 'modulo f' means treating f as zero, i.e. replacing x^{deg f} by whatever f says it equals.
- **Quotient ring R[x]/(f)** — The ring of polynomials taken modulo f: elements are the possible remainders, and multiplication is 'multiply then reduce mod f'. Two polynomials are equal in it exactly when their difference is a multiple of f.
- **Remainder / factor theorem** — For any polynomial A and point a, A mod (x − a) is the constant A(a). Equivalently A(x) = (x−a)Q(x) + A(a). So a single evaluation is literally a reduction modulo a linear polynomial.
- **Coprime elements and ideals** — Two polynomials f, g are coprime (comaximal) if some combination uf + vg = 1. Concretely: if you can write 1 as a polynomial combination of f and g, they share no common factor and CRT applies.
- **Chinese Remainder Theorem (ring form)** — If f = f_1 f_2 with f_1, f_2 coprime, then the map A ↦ (A mod f_1, A mod f_2) is a ring isomorphism R[x]/(f) ≅ R[x]/(f_1) × R[x]/(f_2): the pair of remainders determines A mod f uniquely, and reconstruction is explicit. This is the same theorem as 'x mod 15 ↔ (x mod 3, x mod 5)', with integers replaced by polynomials.
- **Primitive nth root of unity ω** — An element with ω^n = 1 whose powers ω^0, ω^1, …, ω^{n−1} are n distinct values (e.g. ω = e^{2πi/n}). Then x^n − 1 = ∏_{j=0}^{n−1}(x − ω^j), and ω^{n/2} = −1 when n is even.
- **Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT)** — Given coefficients (a_0,…,a_{n−1}) of A(x), the DFT is the vector of values (A(ω^0), A(ω^1), …, A(ω^{n−1})). The inverse DFT recovers the coefficients: a_i = (1/n) Σ_j A(ω^j) ω^{−ij}.
- **FFT butterfly (the recipe he has memorised)** — In the standard radix-2 Cooley–Tukey step (decimation in frequency), you form the pairs a_i + a_{i+n/2} and (a_i − a_{i+n/2})·ω^i for i < n/2, then recurse on the two length-n/2 vectors. The sum/difference pair drawn as an X is called a butterfly; ω^i is the twiddle factor.
- **Lagrange interpolation** — A polynomial of degree < n is determined by its values at n distinct points, via A(x) = Σ_j A(p_j) ∏_{k≠j} (x − p_k)/(p_j − p_k). It is the explicit reconstruction formula for the CRT split into linear factors.
- **Formal derivative fact for products of distinct linear factors** — If f(x) = ∏_k (x − p_k) with distinct p_k, then f′(p_j) = ∏_{k≠j}(p_j − p_k). So the Lagrange denominators are just values of f′.

**The turn:** Evaluating a polynomial at a point is reduction modulo (x − a), so the FFT is just the Chinese Remainder Theorem applied to the factorisation x^n − 1 = (x^{n/2}−1)(x^{n/2}+1): the butterfly's sums and differences are the two remainders, the twiddles are the change of variable, and the inverse transform's 1/n is the CRT reconstruction denominator.
